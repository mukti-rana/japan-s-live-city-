## No photo credits on the home page (rule)

The home page is pictures and type on black. A photographer's name, "Photo:", or a licence is never shown on it, over or beside a picture, in the hero, a chapter, a band, a Discover card or the footer index. Credits live on `/credits`, which lists every home photograph with photographer, licence and source links (CC BY needs exactly that: author, licence, link, "changed"). The only trace on the home page is the one "Photo credits" link in the footer index.

- Anything that prints a credit must carry the attribute `data-photo-credit`. `cinematic.css` hides every `[data-home-root] [data-photo-credit]`, so a photograph added later (or a Discover/Explore card showing someone else's picture) cannot put a name on the home page.
- A new photograph goes in `src/lib/data/homePhotos.ts` with its credit record; `/credits` picks it up on its own.

## Community as a social platform (2026-11-20, approved)

The Community pages are a pitch-black social platform in **both themes** (`data-community-page` on the layout re-applies the dark palette over the light theme and keeps the top bar dark; the page background is dark too so the opening fade never flashes white). The Discover page opens with a centred Playfair heading and a one-line introduction, then the section tabs (Discover, Photos, Reviews, Questions, My Contributions: a pill with a thin amber-pink-violet ribbon slides to the current section, also across page loads), then search and the single filter button. The feed is one centred column (max 720px) of large posts (`FeedPost`: avatar ring, the photograph(s) at their own proportions between 0.9 and 1.6 and at most 78% of the screen height, a swipeable carousel for several photographs, location, serif title, caption, like / comment / share / save) with Topics and Cities kept to two small expandable pills under the search (`QuickBrowse`; each opens its choices below and puts them away again; they are plain links). Likes and saves (`PostReactions`) use the same tables and rules as the post page, with one provider reading the visitor's own likes and saves for the whole page in two requests. **Share your experience** moved out of the header into the **Create dock** (`CommunityDock`): a floating, fully transparent pill (no fill, the page shows through; the label has a soft shadow to read over any photograph) at the foot of the screen with a hairline edge round which one short comet of amber-pink-violet light circles (nothing glows outside the edge), a hand-drawn camera-plus icon in a small gradient disc that tilts on hover, a sheen across the glass, a lift on hover and a press on tap, hidden on forms and single-post pages; it opens the unchanged `/community/new`. Only real data is shown; no post, user, like or review is ever invented. **Not built:** a "Following" section, because the database has no follow relationship; it needs a new table with Row Level Security, which is a database change for the owner to approve.

## The weather card's sky looks like the real one (2026-11-20, approved)

`WeatherSky` draws what is really outside. The **moon** is a textured disc (seas, craters and a bright ray crater, made from noise), lit by the real phase with a soft terminator and a faint earthshine, turned so its bright side faces where the sun is, rising and setting on a real moon's timetable (it can hang pale in the daytime sky), with moonlight lifting the sky and the clouds near it and a ring round it in thin cloud. **Stars** follow a real brightness spread in true colours, sharp points with halos only on the brightest, scintillating, thinning toward the hazy horizon and near the moon, and the field turns with the real time. The **city** puts an orange light dome over the skyline at night (low cloud reflects it), glowing windows and blinking red beacons on the antennas. Thin **cirrus** drifts even in clear weather; by day the sun has a bloom and faint rays and the horizon a pale haze. After dark a clear sky is a **moon** icon and a part-cloudy one a cloud over the moon (`WeatherIcon night`), in the main icon and in each hour of the next-hours strip (judged from that hour against the real sunrise and sunset).
